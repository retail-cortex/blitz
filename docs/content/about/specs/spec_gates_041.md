## 1. Purpose

A team's expectations of its agents ("never touch production", "say how the change was tested") are more dependable checked than hoped for. A rule says one such thing in plain English; a fast model judges it on the prompt before the agent runs, or on the answer and the turn's changes before the turn ends; and the rule says what a failure does.

## 2. Requirements

- **GT-01** A rule file (`*.md` in the rule folders, `.blitz/rules`, `.agents/rules`, `.claude/rules` and the global one; MEM-07) with front matter `when: before_agent` or `when: after_agent` is a **gate**: not an instruction (the memory loader skips it), judged around the turn. Other fields: `action` (`block`, `warn` or `revise`; `warn` when unset), `order` (gates run by order, then name), `agents` (the agents whose turns it gates; none means every agent), `description`. Its body is the rule. A rule without `when` is an instruction rule, unchanged (MEM-07). An unknown `when` or `action`, or an empty gate, is the rule's problem, and it's skipped (`gates.Parse`).
- **GT-02** The judge (`gates.Judge`) asks the suggestions model (else auto mode's, else the workspace's) whether the subject passes the one rule, with a brief to fail only when the rule is clearly broken (a rule that doesn't apply passes), and reads its JSON verdict (`pass` or `fail`, a reason saying what would make it pass). Secrets in the subject are redacted first.
- **GT-03** **Before the agent**: once the prompt is accepted (after the `prompt_submit` hooks), each before-agent gate offered to the active agent judges it, in order. A failing **block** rule refuses the prompt (`*api.BlockedError`, "The rule … stopped this prompt: …"), nothing recorded, the agent not run; **warn** says so in the conversation once the prompt is recorded; **revise** gives the agent the reason with the prompt (`<rules-hook-context>`). Side questions aren't judged.
- **GT-04** **After the agent**: once the turn's passes are done (an approved plan carried out, `stop` hooks and a goal), each after-agent gate offered to the active agent judges the answer (12 KiB) and the diff of the files the latest pass changed (`Checkpoints.LatestTurnDiff`, 12 KiB), in order. A failing **revise** rule sends the agent back with the reasons ("(rules) …" in the transcript, `session.KindRule`), and the rules judge again, twice at most; then "Rules still fail after 2 revisions". **block** marks the answer as failing the rule (an error notice); **warn** a notice. A plan that wasn't carried out isn't judged.
- **GT-05** A rule the judge can't judge (no model, an answer that isn't a verdict) says so ("The rule … couldn't be checked") and lets the turn go on.
- **GT-06** Every verdict goes to the audit log (`audit.KindRule`: the rule, pass, warn, revise, block or unjudged, and the reason).
- **GT-07** `WorkspaceService.ListRules` returns the workspace's rules: gates in the order they run, then instruction rules; each with whether it's editable (in `.agents/rules`). `SaveRule` writes `.agents/rules/<name>.md` from the form, renaming when the name changes, keeping the front matter's other fields (`frontmatter.Edit`); an instruction rule keeps no gate fields, a gate no `paths`; problems (a bad name, when or action, an empty rule, a taken name) come back with nothing written. `DeleteRule` (`RULE_NOT_FOUND`), `ReorderRules` (orders 10, 20, 30… in the order given) and `TryRule` (a sample prompt or answer judged against the rule as written; a judge that fails is a `problem`).
- **GT-08** **Studio › Rules** (`RulesPane`): three lists, **Before the agent** and **After the agent** (each in the order its rules run, numbered, with the action and the agents; dragged, or moved with ↑ ↓, to reorder) and **Instructions** (always, or for the files named); a rule elsewhere than `.agents/rules` is listed with its path and opens read-only. A rule's form slides in over the list as an agent's does: name, what it's for, when (before, after, or an instruction), the action with what it does then, the agents it applies to (or an instruction's files), the rule with **Redraft** (`DraftManifest` kind `rule`), and for a gate **Try it** (a sample, **Check**, the verdict). Back, Cancel and Save; unsaved changes are asked about.
- **GT-09** Each list row is one button that opens the rule beside its own buttons (↑ ↓), never a button inside a button, so each control has its own name.
- **GT-11** **Rules or guardrails**: Studio › Rules and Studio › Guardrails both have an (i) beside the page's title (`GatesTitle`) that shows the same side-by-side comparison under it, hidden at first since vertical space is dear (`RulesOrGuardrails`: what each is for, when it runs, what it checks, what a failure does, an example; the page shown marked "this page") and a button to the other: rules check the work against the team's expectations, at the turn's edges (the prompt before, the answer and changes after), and can send the work back; guardrails are for safety and limits (instructions hidden in fetched pages or files, secrets in answers, spending, risky commands) and watch every step, built in or the user's own, blocking, redacting or warning, never revising. A line under it says when the other is the one to use ("About safety rather than quality? It's a guardrail."). A rule's form says the same in a line (a gate: "if it's about safety rather than quality, make it a guardrail"; an instruction rule: it isn't checked).
- **GT-10** `pkg/engine/frontmatter` edits Markdown front matter for Studio's forms (rules, skills): fields set, or removed when empty, every other field kept in its order.

### Guardrails

- **GT-20** A guardrail is `.agents/guardrails/<name>.md` (or `~/.blitz/guardrails/<name>.md`, for every workspace; a workspace's file wins over a global one of the same name), with front matter `stage` (`input`: the prompt; `tool_result`: what a tool returns, before the agent reads it; `output`: the agent's answer; `action`: each tool call, before it runs), `action` (`block`, `warn` or `redact`; what the stage allows), `agents` (none: every agent), `tools` (for `tool_result` and `action`; none: every tool, or for prompt injection the tools that bring in outside content: `web_fetch`, `web_search`, `browser` and MCP tools), `description`, `enabled` (`false` switches it off, its settings kept), and either `builtin: <id>` or a body: the user's own check in plain English. An unknown stage, action or built-in, or a custom guardrail with no body, is its problem, and it's skipped (`gates.ParseGuardrail`). Guardrails are listed built-ins first, then by stage and name.
- **GT-21** Three **built-ins** (`gates.Builtins`), **on by default**: without a file, each runs with its defaults (`gates.BuiltinDefault`). Its file (named after it, so it's versioned with the project) changes its settings, or switches it off with `enabled: false`; deleting the file puts it back to its defaults. The built-ins: **prompt injection** (`prompt_injection`, tool results, `warn` or `block`; patterns for text aimed at the agent, `judge: true` to have the judge confirm a match first), **secrets and personal data** (`secrets`, answers, `redact`, `warn` or `block`; the redactor's credential patterns, and with `pii: true` email addresses, phone numbers, Luhn-valid card numbers and social security numbers, `redact.PII`) and **limits** (`limits`, actions, `block`; `max_cost_usd`, `max_turns`, `max_files_changed`, `allowed_domains`, `commands`).
- **GT-22** **Limits**: a turn's model calls and spending are capped at the guardrail's `max_turns` and `max_cost_usd` (the lower of it and the turn's own). Before a tool runs, a URL in its arguments off `allowed_domains` (a domain allows its subdomains), a shell command matching a `commands` glob (`*` for anything, against the whole command), or a write once the turn has changed `max_files_changed` files is refused with the reason ("🛡 The guardrail limits stopped this: …").
- **GT-23** **Prompt injection**, on what a checked tool returns: **warn** gives the agent the result with a caution that it's content, not instructions (`guardrail` in the result), and says so in the chat; **block** withholds the result (the agent is told it was withheld and not to follow it) and says so.
- **GT-24** **Secrets** in the answer, as it streams and in full: **redact** masks them ("🛡 … masked secrets or personal data in the answer"), **block** withholds the text, **warn** says the answer shows what looks like one. Each guardrail's notice comes once a turn.
- **GT-25** **Custom guardrails** are judged (`gates.JudgeGuardrail`, the rules' judge with a safety brief): `input` on the prompt before the turn (after the input built-ins, before the before-agent rules: GT-03), `action` on the tool and its arguments, `tool_result` on the result, `output` on the whole answer. A failing **block** refuses the prompt or the call, withholds the result, or withholds the answer, with the reason; **warn** says so. A custom guardrail that **can't be checked** (no model, no verdict) **refuses** what it guards when it blocks, unlike a rule; when it warns, it says it couldn't check.
- **GT-26** The engine calls the guardrails through `runtime.Guard` (`WithGuard`): `ToolCall` before a tool runs, `ToolResult` after, `Output` on the model's text; a sub-agent's calls are guarded by its parent's turn's guardrails. Every decision goes to the audit log (`audit.KindGuardrail`).
- **GT-27** `WorkspaceService.ListGuardrails` returns the built-ins (each as it runs: its file, or its defaults with no path; `off` when switched off) and the workspace's own guardrails. `SaveGuardrail` writes `.agents/guardrails/<name>.md` from the form (a built-in under its id, edited in place when it's there, `enabled: false` when it's off; renaming when a custom one's name changes; other front matter kept, GT-10), problems coming back with nothing written. `DeleteGuardrail` (a built-in's file: back to its defaults; `GUARDRAIL_NOT_FOUND`) and `TryGuardrail` (a sample prompt, result, answer or action checked as the guardrail is written: the patterns for the built-ins, the judge for custom ones).
- **GT-28** **Studio › Guardrails** (`GuardrailsPane`): **Built in**, on unless switched off, each with what it watches, its action, a switch (off writes `enabled: false` in its file, keeping its settings; on removes it) and its settings; and **Your own**. A guardrail's form slides in over the list (GT-08's layout): a built-in's switch beside its title, its settings (the action; personal data too; confirm with a model; the limits) and the agents it guards, and **Reset to defaults** (asked first) once it has a file; a custom one's name, what it's for, what it checks, the action with what it does then, its tools, agents and what to catch, with **Draft** or **Redraft** (`DraftManifest` kind `guardrail`); **Try it** with a sample (not for limits). Back, Cancel and Save, unsaved changes asked about.
- **GT-29** The engine's notices and the page's text are in every locale (`guardrail.*`, `desktop.guardrails.*`).

## 3. Known gaps

A custom `output` guardrail judges the answer once it's complete, so a streamed answer is shown before it's withheld. Limits count the files the turn changed by writes the engine sees, not by shell commands.
